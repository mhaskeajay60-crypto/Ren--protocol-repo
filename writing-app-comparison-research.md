# Writing-App Product Gap Research

## Purpose

This note supports an honest comparison of The Ren Protocol with established writing applications. It records patterns to adapt, not functions to copy. The Ren Protocol remains browser-local by default and keeps author approval ahead of AI, filing, and group-canon actions.

## Sudowrite — Guided Story Flow

Sudowrite presents its Story Bible as a connected progression from a manually captured brain dump through synopsis, characters, worldbuilding, outline, scenes, and draft. It emphasizes that the writer can manually populate any field and revise AI suggestions. The useful pattern for The Ren Protocol is **one visible story path** that connects Vault → World → Plan → Write, not automatic AI prose or uncontrolled context sending.

Source: [Sudowrite, “What is Story Bible?”](https://docs.sudowrite.com/using-sudowrite/1ow1qkGqof9rtcyGnrWUBS/what-is-story-bible/jmWepHcQdJetNrE991fjJC)

## Novelcrafter — Flexible Codex and Progressions

Novelcrafter centers an integrated codex, links story elements to manuscript mentions, supports custom metadata, and tracks changes over time through progressions. Its strongest relevant lesson is that a world bible becomes valuable when it is **visible while writing** and when changing character/location facts can be reviewed in time order. The Ren Protocol already has local Masterbook, custom stats, links, continuity tools, and history; its gap is a simpler Context Tray and a clear, author-approved character/location progression view.

Source: [Novelcrafter, “The Codex”](https://www.novelcrafter.com/features/codex)

## Plottr — Purposeful Planning Views

Plottr distinguishes visual timelines, scene cards, plot lines, custom scene attributes, filters, and templates. The key lesson is not to put every planning mode on one phone screen. A writer should select a purpose—timeline, scenes, plotline, or character arc—then see only the necessary controls. The new Pocket Planner follows this direction, but needs one reliable Timeline/Arc screen with linked scenes and chapters.

Source: [Plottr Features](https://plottr.com/features/)

## Scrivener — One Project, Multiple Working Modes

Scrivener combines manuscript, research, outliner, corkboard, composition focus, targets, snapshots, and exports in one project. Its useful pattern is a stable project spine: sections can be viewed as a manuscript, a compact outline, or cards without becoming separate confusing tools. The Ren Protocol’s next improvement should provide a single Chapter Hub with prose, brief, scenes, notes, versions, and export actions in one place.

Source: [Scrivener Overview](https://www.literatureandlatte.com/scrivener/overview)

## World Anvil — Permission Boundaries

World Anvil separates draft creation from ownership/edit/publish powers. The useful pattern for The Ren Protocol is already present: writers can propose, the Ruler controls official canon, and private material stays local. The product should make this boundary clearer in plain language rather than add new roles prematurely.

Source: [World Anvil, “Feature Guide to Co-Authors”](https://www.worldanvil.com/learn/access-rights/co-authors)
